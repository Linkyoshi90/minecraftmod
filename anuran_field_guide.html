Bullywugs & Mucus Toads

Two frog-and-toad monster-girl lineages. Each will split into species the way the harpies did, but they start from one shared body plan: Anura, the order every frog and toad belongs to. This guide defines that shared core first. Species come later.

The central similarity

Born in water. Bound to it.

Harpies are defined by the sky. Anurans are defined by water: every Bullywug and every Mucus Toad started as a tadpole, and every adult still needs wet skin. That one fact drives most of their behaviour, so it is the shared core both lineages build on.

  • One life cycleSpawn, tadpole, metamorphosis, adult. Shared by both lineages and every future species.
  • One needSkin moisture. Water, rain and mud refill it; sun, heat and the Nether drain it.
  • One toolkitA long tongue, a strong leap, a croaking chorus.
  • One difference that mattersSkin. Smooth frog skin dries fast and keeps Bullywugs at the water's edge. Warty toad skin holds water and lets Mucus Toads roam.

The life cycle (shared)

The loop mirrors vanilla Minecraft frogs on purpose: frogspawn, bucketable tadpoles, and a variant chosen by the biome the tadpole grows up in. Players already understand it, and it gives these two a hatchling-to-bond path the harpies don't have.

1. SpawnAfter a chorus night, a pair may leave a clutch in shallow, still water. Bullywug spawn is a floating clump; toad spawn is a long string wound around reeds and seagrass, like in real frogs and toads. Rare, like harpy eggs.
2. TadpoleTiny, aquatic, harmless. Can be caught in a bucket like a vanilla tadpole. Grows over a few in-game days; feeding it (slimeballs, kelp) speeds that up. Owl harpies and fish-eating mobs are its predators.
3. MetamorphosisThe tadpole crawls ashore. Two things are decided here: the variant, from the biome it grows up in (warm, temperate or cold, the same rule as vanilla frogs), and its keeper. A tadpole raised in a pond the player built treats that pond as home and the player as familiar.
4. AdultLives on land, never far from water. Wild adults also spawn naturally in their biomes, so the life cycle is a bonus path and not the only source of the population.

Shared systems

Every system below exists in both lineages. The lineage only changes the numbers and the flavour, the same way species variants re-skin a harpy without changing the model.

MoistureA hidden value from wet to dry. Refills in water, rain and mud, and near a filled cauldron. Drains in sunlight, in hot or dry biomes, and very fast in the Nether. When dry, she slows down, looks dull and heads for the nearest water. Being dry never kills her; it just sends her home.
The tongueA reach attack of about 4 blocks. It catches food (insects, small slimes, magma cubes: eating one leaves a froglight, like vanilla frogs) and pulls dropped items in. What she does with the item is the lineage's personality.
Leap & swimFast swimmers who never drown. A strong jump and little fall damage. Out of water they move in bursts rather than a steady walk.
The chorusAt dusk, in rain and right after rain, they croak with an inflating throat sac. Nearby individuals join in and settle into one rhythm. The chorus is how the player finds them, and a chorus night is when spawn gets laid.
Wetting as kindnessThe shared bond action: splash a dry one with a water bucket or water bottle, or build her a pond. It is the anuran version of feeding a harpy, and it plugs into the same affection system.
Cold sleepIn snowy biomes, or when it snows, she digs into mud or a pond bed and sleeps until it warms up (this is called brumation). Wake her early and she is groggy and grumpy.

Where the lineages split

Real frogs and toads are the same animal with different skin, and that is how these two diverge. Each row is a shared system; the cells say how each lineage turns it.

Lineage profiles

Swamp & riverbankSocial · boisterous

Bullywug

HabitatSwamps, mangrove swamps, rivers and ponds with lily pads

TemperamentLoud, boastful, pushy, secretly easy to scare

Ecology & behaviourLives in noisy groups at the water's edge. Sits on lily pads, holds bellow contests, snatches anything shiny or edible with her tongue, and leaps into the water the moment a fight turns against her.

Player interactionStand your ground and she backs down. Keep her pond wet and stocked with fish and she becomes a loud, loyal pond-mate who shows off for you.

NameFrom the frog-folk of tabletop fantasy (Dungeons & Dragons): "bully" plus "pollywog", an old word for tadpole.

DangerModerate in a group; low when you meet one alone.

Damp woodland & cavesSolitary · unbothered

Mucus Toad

HabitatDamp forest floor, lush caves, farm and garden edges, riverbanks

TemperamentPlacid, slow, stubborn, sleepy

Ecology & behaviourWanders far from water, eats insects and small slimes, digs in under soft soil during the day and comes out at dusk and in rain. Her mucus makes her slippery and bitter, so most predators leave her alone.

Player interactionLet her settle near your crops and she keeps pests down. Shed mucus could be a useful material (a sticky ingredient, or a salve).

NameFrom the Monster Girl Encyclopedia. The source is erotic, so following the "mainly fantasy" tone rule, her mucus here is defensive and practical.

DangerLow; nausea for anyone who attacks her.

Ties to the harpies

One world, one food web. These are small hooks, not new systems.

Owl harpyThe field guide already lists frogs as owl prey. Owls hunt tadpoles and Bullywugs at night, but learn to avoid Mucus Toads after one bitter bite.
Crow & seagullFellow thieves. A Bullywug with a stolen item and a crow harpy who wants it could be a fun ambient scene.
Shared codeThe affection, home position and synced-action plumbing in AbstractHarpy fits here too, as a shared parent or a sibling AbstractAnuran. Vanilla Frog and Tadpole already have tongue, variant and growth code to learn from.

Species to come

Not designed yet. Real animals that could become species later, listed only so the shared core leaves room for them.

Bullywug candidates

  • Tree frogSmall and sticky-toed: climbs trees and walls.
  • Poison dart frogBright warning colours; jungle.
  • Glass frogSee-through skin; rare.

Mucus Toad candidates

  • Common toadThe baseline: brown, warty, unbothered.
  • Cane toadBig, toxic, eats anything.
  • Fire-bellied toadFlashes a red belly when scared; cold biomes.
  • NatterjackSandy coasts; runs instead of hopping.
  • Surinam toadFlat; carries her eggs in her back. Odd but memorable.

Design decisions log

Added 2026-10-18.

BriefTwo frog-creature lineages, the Bullywug and the Mucus Toad, in the spirit of the harpy guide. Each will get species like the harpies did; for now the focus is on what they have in common.
InterpretationThe central similarity is read as the amphibian life cycle plus the need for water, with skin as the one axis where the lineages split. This is a proposal, not a decision.
ToneSame rule as the harpies: mainly fantasy. The Mucus Toad's erotic source material is reworked into defensive, practical mucus.

Open questions

  • Is the life cycle and water the similarity you had in mind, or did you mean something else (shared looks, a shared society, a shared mechanic)?
  • Wildlife or tribe? Bullywugs in tabletop games live in tribes with chiefs. The harpy guide says ecology, not civilization. A pond "pecking order" (the loudest wins the pond) is the middle ground used above.
  • Can a player-raised tadpole be any lineage, or only the lineage of the spawn it hatched from? (Above: the spawn decides.)
  • The mod id is harpies. If anurans join, does the mod need a broader name?
  • Should drying out ever hurt, or only slow her down? (Above: never lethal.)
